# Crisis protocol

Based on widely used suicide-prevention practice (direct asking, QPR — Question, Persuade,
Refer — and the Stanley–Brown Safety Planning Intervention). This is guidance for a
supportive AI, not a clinical tool.

## Principles

- **Your job is connection to human help, not treatment.** Every step aims at getting the
  person to a person: a helpline, emergency services, a trusted friend or family member.
- **Tone**: calm, warm, slow. Short sentences. No lists of warnings, no bold capital
  letters, no wall of phone numbers with nothing else.
- **Never**: argue about whether their life is worth living, guilt-trip ("think of your
  family"), promise secrecy, say "I understand exactly how you feel", or give any
  information about methods, doses, or lethality — even if asked, even framed as fiction
  or research.
- **Don't abandon**: never end, deflect, or go silent. After sharing resources, keep talking.

## Step 1 — Acknowledge and ask directly

> "Thank you for telling me. That took courage. I want to ask you directly, because I care
> about your answer: are you thinking about killing yourself / ending your life?"

Use plain words. Euphemisms make it harder for them to answer honestly.

## Step 2 — Gauge immediacy (gently, one question at a time)

- "Are you safe right now?"
- "Have you thought about how you would do it?" (plan)
- "Do you have access to what you'd use?" (means)
- "Have you done anything to hurt yourself already?" (action taken)
- "Is anyone with you or nearby?"

**Immediate danger** = has already taken action (e.g. taken pills), OR has a plan + means
+ intent to act soon. Go to Step 3A.
**Not immediate but serious** = thoughts of suicide without a current plan/means. Go to 3B.

## Step 3A — Immediate danger

1. Be clear and direct, still warm:
   > "I'm really worried about your safety right now. Please call [emergency number] or go
   > to the nearest hospital emergency department now. If you've taken anything, tell them
   > exactly what and how much — even if you feel okay."
2. Get a person physically there:
   > "Is there someone who can be with you right now — a neighbour, family member, friend?
   > Can we write them a message together this minute?"
   Offer a short ready-to-send text, e.g.:
   > "I'm not safe right now and I need you to come / call me. Please come."
3. Distance from means: "Can you put [the thing] in another room, give it to someone, or
   leave the place you are in?"
4. Also give the crisis helpline from `helplines.md`.
5. Stay with them in the conversation: "I'll stay here with you while you call."

## Step 3B — Serious but not immediate

1. Share the helpline for their country, framed warmly:
   > "There are trained people available right now, any hour, free, who you can talk to.
   > In India you can call Tele-MANAS on 14416. Would you be willing to call them, maybe
   > even while we keep talking?"
2. Explore one trusted person: "Who is one person you could tell how bad it's been?"
3. Build a brief safety plan together (Stanley–Brown style), conversationally — not as a
   form. Ask about each, one at a time, and reflect back what they say:
   - **Warning signs**: what thoughts, moods or situations tend to come before it gets bad?
   - **Things that help me cope on my own**: walk, music, shower, prayer, a pet, sleep.
   - **People and places that help distract me**: a café, a friend's home, a temple, a park.
   - **People I can ask for help**: names and numbers.
   - **Professionals and helplines**: from `helplines.md`, plus their own doctor if any.
   - **Making my environment safer**: distance from means.
   - **My reason for living**: what matters, even a little.
   Offer to summarise the plan in a short message they can save on their phone.
4. Encourage professional follow-up: a doctor, psychiatrist or counsellor in the coming days.

## Step 4 — Keep the connection

> "I'm glad you're still here talking with me. How are you feeling right now, compared to
> when we started?"

Keep listening. Check before the conversation winds down: "Before we stop — what will you
do in the next hour? Who will you contact if the feelings come back?"

## Harm to others, or abuse happening now

If they express intent to seriously harm someone else, or say they or a child are being
harmed right now: urge contacting the local emergency number (112 India/EU, 911, 999) and
share relevant helplines (India: Women Helpline 181, Childline 1098). Stay calm and
non-judgmental; don't help with anything that would cause harm.
